Leapfrog

Feynman's "little trick" with $\epsilon/2$ is actually called the Leapfrog method (or Leapfrog integration). Unlike the first method introduced (which is named Euler integration by the way), Leapfrog is stable for any repeating (oscillatory) motion (if the timestep $\epsilon$ satisfies some conditions, namely $\epsilon \lt \frac{2}{\omega}$, where $\omega$ is a property that defines how much time the motion takes to "repeat" and return to its initial state).
